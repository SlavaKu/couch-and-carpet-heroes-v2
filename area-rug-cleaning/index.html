Inspection

Every Rug Is Inspected Before Cleaning

Rugs are not all the same, so the cleaning plan should not be guessed.

Material and condition guide the method

Before cleaning, we look at fiber type, construction, backing, fringe, color stability, soil level, stains, odor concerns and signs of wear. This helps us choose a practical cleaning method for the rug rather than treating every piece the same way.

We also explain realistic expectations. Some old stains, dye movement, sun fading, worn fibers or prior chemical damage may remain visible after cleaning.

Stains

Food, Drink and Everyday Spots

Fresh spills are often easier to improve than older stains that have settled into the fibers.

Blot First

Use a clean white towel and gentle pressure. Avoid hard scrubbing, which can spread the spot and rough up fibers.

Avoid Harsh Products

Bleach, mixed cleaners and strong household products can cause color loss or permanent damage.

Send Photos

Photos of the full rug, stain close-ups and backing help us understand the best next step.

Pets and odors

Pet Accidents, Odors and High-Traffic Areas

Pets and daily traffic can affect both the look and smell of an area rug.

Pet urine and odor

Many pet accident spots can improve, especially when handled quickly. Deep or repeated contamination may not fully disappear.

Tracked-in soil

Entry rugs and living room rugs collect grit that can dull fibers over time.

Stale room smells

Odors from pets, spills and everyday use can settle into rugs and make the room feel less fresh.

Color and dye caution

Some rugs have dyes that need special care. Inspection helps reduce avoidable risk.

Fringe and edges

Fringe, binding and worn edges are reviewed so we can be careful around weaker areas.

Honest outcomes

We do not promise complete stain removal. The goal is a careful improvement with clear expectations.

Protection

Rug Protection After Cleaning

Small habits can help protect your rug between professional cleanings.

Use a Rug Pad

A good rug pad helps reduce slipping, wear and grinding against the floor.

Rotate the Rug

Rotating a rug helps balance sunlight, traffic lanes and furniture pressure marks.

Act on Spills Quickly

Quick blotting and simple care can reduce the chance of a permanent mark.

Replace or clean

When To Clean vs Replace an Area Rug

Cleaning is often worth trying when the rug is structurally sound, but replacement can make more sense when damage is severe.

Clean Area RugReplace Area Rug
Best when the rug has soil, traffic lanes, light odors or surface stains.Better for severe fiber loss, unstable backing or heavy permanent dye damage.
Helpful when the rug still fits the room and has useful life left.May be better when the rug is unsafe, brittle, torn or no longer lays flat.
Lower cost and less waste than replacing a rug that can still be used.Necessary when cleaning cannot solve structural damage or severe contamination.

Schedule

How Often Should Area Rugs Be Professionally Cleaned?

The best timing depends on foot traffic, pets, kids, allergies, rug material and where the rug sits in the home.

Simple timing guide

  • Typical homes: every 12 to 18 months
  • Homes with pets or kids: every 6 to 12 months, or sooner after accidents
  • Entry rugs and high-traffic rugs: every 6 to 12 months
  • Delicate or valuable rugs: inspect first and clean only with the right method
  • Rental and furnished properties: between tenants or before showings when needed

Quick Answers

Quick Answers

Short answers for common area rug cleaning decisions.

Can you clean wool rugs?

Yes, after inspection. Wool needs fiber-aware care and realistic expectations around dyes and older stains.

Can all stains come out?

No. Some stains improve, while others may be permanent because of dye, age, chemicals or fiber damage.

Can odors be removed?

Many odors improve. Deep pet contamination or repeated accidents may not fully disappear.

Should I send photos?

Yes. Include the full rug, close-ups of spots, the backing and any labels if available.

Can cleaning extend rug life?

Cleaning can remove abrasive soil and help the rug look better longer when the rug is still in good condition.

Do delicate rugs need special care?

Yes. Delicate rugs should be inspected before cleaning because material, age and dyes matter.

FAQ

Area Rug Cleaning FAQ

Helpful details customers often ask before scheduling rug cleaning.

Do you inspect every rug before cleaning?

Yes. Every rug is inspected before cleaning so we can choose a method based on the material, construction, color stability, soil level and condition.

Can all rug stains be removed?

No. Many stains can improve, but some marks may be permanent depending on fiber type, dye, age, previous products and damage.

Can you clean wool rugs?

Yes. Wool rugs need careful inspection and a fiber-appropriate method because dye stability, texture and age can affect the cleaning plan.

Can you help with pet accidents and rug odors?

Many pet spots and odors can improve, especially when treated quickly. Repeated or deep contamination may need extra care and may not disappear completely.

How often should area rugs be professionally cleaned?

Many rugs benefit from professional cleaning every 12 to 18 months. Homes with pets, kids, allergies or heavy traffic may need cleaning every 6 to 12 months.

Should I clean or replace my rug?

Cleaning is often worth trying when the rug is structurally sound. Replacement may be better for severe fiber loss, unstable backing, heavy dye damage or major permanent damage.
